Search for "ATS" and you will find dramatic claims about three-quarters of CVs being rejected by robots before a human sees them. The reality is more mundane, and more useful: applicant tracking systems are databases with a parser bolted on, and their job is to extract your information accurately. Most "rejections" are a human filtering a search result.

That said, a CV that parses badly is a real disadvantage. Here is what actually matters.

## What an ATS does with your file

When you upload a CV, the system tries to turn it into structured fields: name, email, phone, employers, job titles, dates, education, skills. If the parse fails or half-fills the fields, a recruiter scanning the database may never surface your profile.

## The formatting rules that matter most

- **Use real text.** A PDF exported from a text-based editor keeps selectable text. A scan, a screenshot pasted into a document, or text-as-image does not.
- **Keep a conventional structure.** Standard section headings — Experience, Education, Skills — parse better than creative ones like "My Journey".
- **Single column is safest.** Multi-column layouts can be read in the wrong order by older parsers. Two columns usually works; test it if you can.
- **Avoid text in headers and footers.** Many parsers ignore them entirely. If your phone number lives in the header, it may vanish.
- **Do not use tables for layout.** A table used to arrange skills into columns can scramble into nonsense.
- **Skip icons as labels.** A phone icon instead of the word "Phone" may not be understood.
- **Use standard date formats.** "March 2021 – Present" beats "03/21–now" and beats "2021–".

## The keyword question

Most ATS search works by keyword. That means the words in your CV should overlap with the words in the job description — honestly. If the advert asks for "project management" and you ran projects, say "project management", not "ran stuff".

Spelling matters here too. If the advert uses American spelling and you use British, consider covering both in the skills section where it reads naturally.

## A five-minute self-test

1. Open your exported PDF.
2. Select all and copy.
3. Paste into a plain text editor.

If the paste contains your name, contact details, job titles, dates and skills in a sensible order, you are in good shape. If it comes out as gibberish, missing, or empty, fix the layout before applying.
