Recruiters spend somewhere between six and thirty seconds on a first pass. In that window they are not reading your CV — they are scanning it. What they scan for is evidence: numbers, outcomes, and signs that you have done this job before.

Most CVs lose this fight because their bullets describe duties rather than results.

## The problem with duty bullets

Compare these two lines:

- **Duty:** "Responsible for managing the company social media accounts."
- **Result:** "Grew Instagram engagement 34% in six months by shifting to a three-posts-a-week video format."

The first tells a reader what your job description said. The second tells them what you are capable of. Only one of those is useful when they are deciding whether to interview you.

## A formula you can reuse

A strong bullet usually has four moving parts:

1. **Action** — the thing you did, in a strong verb.
2. **Context** — what it was for, or the scale involved.
3. **Result** — the measurable change that followed.
4. **Method** (optional) — how you did it, if the how is impressive.

"**Cut** (action) checkout abandonment **for a 200k-user app** (context) **by 18%** (result) **by rebuilding the payment flow around saved cards** (method)."

You do not need all four every time. You do need at least a result.

## Where numbers come from

If you are staring at a bullet wondering what number to attach, try these angles:

- **Money** — revenue, savings, budget managed, cost reduced.
- **Time** — hours saved, turnaround improved, deadlines met.
- **Volume** — users, tickets, records, events, clients, students.
- **Reach** — people trained, teams supported, audience size.
- **Quality** — error rates, satisfaction scores, retention, uptime.

An honest estimate beats a vague claim. "Roughly 40 tickets a day" is fine. "Handled tickets" is not.

## Verbs worth borrowing

Cut the phrases that hedge. Replace them:

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Responsible for | Led / Owned / Ran |
| Helped with | Supported / Contributed to |
| Worked on | Built / Shipped / Delivered |
| Assisted in | Partnered with |
| Tasked with | Managed / Coordinated |

## How many bullets per role

Two to five for most roles. Three is a good default. For your current job, favour the top end; for something a decade ago, one line is plenty.

If a role genuinely was routine — a summer job, a short contract — it is fine to give it a single line and move on. Padding it with invented achievements reads worse than brevity.

## A quick self-check

Read each bullet and ask:

- Does it start with a verb rather than a noun phrase?
- Is there a result or a number in it?
- Could a person unfamiliar with my company understand it?
- Have I used the same verb twice in this section?

Fix whatever fails and you will have a stronger CV than most of the pile.
