Tailoring sounds like work, and for most people it means rewriting the whole document for every application. It should not. If your master CV is already strong, tailoring is a fifteen-minute edit: swap the order, mirror the vocabulary, cut what does not apply.

## Step 1: Turn the advert into a checklist

Read the posting with a pen and pull out every explicit ask. Separate them into two lists:

- **Requirements** — "must", "essential", "you will have".
- **Nice to haves** — "desirable", "bonus", "exposure to".

You will usually end up with eight to twelve items. That list is your marking scheme. Anything on it that appears nowhere in your CV is a gap you either fill or consciously accept.

## Step 2: Mirror their language

This is not about stuffing keywords. It is about using the same words a human reader (and a parser) expects.

If the advert says "stakeholder management" twelve times and your CV says "worked with clients", rewrite the bullet. Same work, matching vocabulary. If they say "React" and you wrote "modern JavaScript frameworks", name React.

> Rule of thumb: if a term is central to the advert and genuinely describes something you did, use their word for it.

Do not claim tools you have not used. Mirroring is about naming your real experience in their language, not inventing experience.

## Step 3: Reorder, do not rewrite

Your best material already exists. Tailoring mostly means moving it.

- **Summary:** rewrite the first two lines to name the role and the two strongest matches.
- **Experience:** keep every role, but move the most relevant bullets to the top of each list.
- **Skills:** reorder so the ones from their requirements list come first.
- **Sections:** if they care about certifications and you have them, move that section above projects.

In CV Maker, drag-and-drop does this in seconds — reorder bullets within a role, or reorder whole sections.

## Step 4: Cut what dilutes

A tailored CV is also a shorter one. Remove:

- Bullets that describe work unrelated to this role.
- A whole section if it does not support the application.
- Anything that is only there because it was there last time.

## Step 5: Run the thirty-second check

Hand your CV to someone (or read it yourself) with the advert next to it. For each requirement they listed, can the reader find evidence in under thirty seconds? If not, move the evidence higher or make it more explicit.

## What changes between applications

| Element | Changes per application? |
| --- | --- |
| Contact details | No |
| Employment history | No |
| Bullet order within a role | Yes |
| Summary | Yes |
| Skills order | Yes |
| Section order | Sometimes |
| Accent colour / template | Rarely (keep it professional) |

## Keep a master copy

The trick that makes all of this fast is having one complete master CV with everything in it. Tailoring is subtraction and reordering from that master, never a rewrite. Duplicate the CV in your dashboard, make the fifteen-minute edit, and apply.

That way you keep the depth for the roles that need it, and you never lose a bullet you might want back.
